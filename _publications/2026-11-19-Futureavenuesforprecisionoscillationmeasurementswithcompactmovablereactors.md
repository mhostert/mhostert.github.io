abstract: "No planned neutrino experiment is expected to surpass Daya Bay’s precision on $\\thone$. Doing so requires innovative strategies to reduce statistical and systematic uncertainties. In this study, we consider a futuristic program with a movable reactor antineutrino source placed within short distances ($L\\lesssim 3\\,\\km$) from the Jiangmen Underground Neutrino Observatory (JUNO) detector. Due to the large fiducial mass of JUNO, even a relatively small reactor can produce a statistically significant number of events over a few years to probe the $\\lesssim 8.7\\% \\,\\nuebar$ disappearance set by $\\thone$. We consider two possibilities: a $\\mathcal{O}(20\\mwth)$ microreactor in alcoves of the access tunnel to the underground laboratory, and a $\\mathcal{O}(400 \\mwth)$ unit on the surface. In both cases the reactor is observed by a small flux monitor detector near the source, while JUNO measures the antineutrino flux at two baselines, suppressing correlated flux and detector systematics. Using the measured Daya Bay per-isotope spectra and its covariance, we model a high-assay low-enriched uranium (HALEU) core for the microreactor and a low-enriched uranium (LEU) core for the surface unit, and discuss various potential sources of systematic uncertainties and their impact on the precision of $\\thone$. The underground microreactor could improve on JUNO's reach by a factor of three to four, to $\\relsth \\simeq 4\\%$ over a decade, while the surface reactor would reach the present global-fit precision, $\\relsth=2.5\\%$, in about three years, provided the emission rate is monitored across reactor relocations to the sub-percent level."
